Hospitalised road traffic injuries in Australia, 2011–2021

Over 39,000 people were hospitalised after road crashes in 2021. This story follows how that number changed, who was injured, and where.

Counts are not adjusted for population or travel. Reporting changes in Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017) affect comparisons over time.
Design note: one scrolling page, one section per research question. Caveats appear up front so users read every chart with them in mind.

Question 1

How did hospitalisations change from 2011 to 2021?

Counts rose from 34,033 to 39,505, peaked in 2019, then dipped in 2020.

Line chart: national count by year

Question 2

Which road users drove the change?

Pedal cyclists rose the most (+2,772); pedestrians fell (−420).

Question 4

Who was hospitalised, by age and sex?

Males aged 40–64 were the largest group in 2021 (8,702). Older age groups grew.

Question 5

Hospitalisations of First Nations Peoples

Counts rose from 1,150 in 2011 to 2,214 in 2021, peaking at 2,290 in 2020. Motorcyclist hospitalisations rose from 187 to 520.

Line chart: annual count
100% stacked bars: road-user shares
Some small counts are marked “n.p.” (not published) by the source and are shown as gaps, not zeros. Road-user categories were combined to match the national data.
Shares (not raw counts) are compared with national data, to avoid implying group-level risk. Wording is descriptive and avoids deficit framing.
